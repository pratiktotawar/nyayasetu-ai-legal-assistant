2. Facts of the case
--------------------
1. In March 2024 (approx.), I placed an order for refurbished phones on PhoneBazaar wholesale, sold by PhoneBazaar Distributors (Order ID: [MISSING: Order ID]) and paid ₹1,20,000.
2. The product was delivered on 10 March 2024. After delivery it developed a defect. In my words: “I run a small mobile repair shop. In March 2024 I bought 10 refurbished phones worth ₹1,20,000 from an online wholesaler, PhoneBazaar, to resell.”
3. I raised the matter with the seller/platform on 20 March 2024. What I did: “I emailed them on 20 March 2024 and they refused, saying refurbished stock is sold as-is.”
4. The response I received: “Four of them were defective and the seller refused to refund.”
5. As of 7 November 2026, the matter remains unresolved and I have not received the refund of ₹48,000 I asked for.

3. Grounds
----------
1. The goods supplied suffer from a defect within the meaning of Section 2(10) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, which is a ground of complaint under Section 2(6)(ii).
2. Under Rule 6(3) of the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020, a seller on a marketplace shall not refuse to take back goods or refund the consideration where the goods are defective. Where the platform itself is the seller, Rule 7(4) imposes the same duty.
3. I submit that the refusal or failure to take back the defective goods and refund the price within the applicable period may amount to an unfair trade practice under Section 2(47)(viii) of the Consumer Protection Act, 2019.
