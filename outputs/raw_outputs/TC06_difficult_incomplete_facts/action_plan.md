# What should I do next? (raw output)

## Step 1: Preserve your evidence  [todo]
**What to do:** Save copies of everything now, outside the app: invoice, payment proof, order page, photos. Also try to obtain: Invoice / bill, Payment proof (receipt, bank/UPI statement), Warranty card / warranty terms, Technician / service-centre report (job sheet), Photographs of the product / damage, Complaint / ticket reference number. Keep the product and its packaging; do not repair or dispose of it.
**Why it matters:** Apps can change order pages and listings, and chats can disappear. Whoever decides your complaint will rely on documents, not memory.
**Official website:** —
**Evidence to keep:** Invoice / bill, Payment proof (receipt, bank/UPI statement), Warranty card / warranty terms, Technician / service-centre report (job sheet), Photographs of the product / damage, Complaint / ticket reference number

## Step 2: Escalate in writing to the seller and the platform's grievance officer  [partly_done]
**What to do:** You have already complained. Now send the formal written complaint (NyayaSetu draft B) to the platform's grievance officer, quoting your earlier ticket number, and copy the seller (draft A).
**Why it matters:** A written complaint creates a dated record and gives the other side a chance to fix the problem. The E-Commerce Rules require the platform's grievance officer to acknowledge a complaint within 48 hours and redress it within one month — these are duties on the platform.
**Official website:** Grievance officer details are displayed on the platform's own website/app
**Evidence to keep:** Copy of what you sent and the date, Acknowledgement / ticket number

## Step 3: Keep every reference number and reply  [todo]
**What to do:** Write down a simple log: date, how you contacted them, ticket number, name of the agent, what they said. Save emails as PDF.
**Why it matters:** These references prove you tried to resolve the matter and show how long the other side took.
**Official website:** —
**Evidence to keep:** Contact log, Ticket numbers, Replies (emails / chat screenshots)

## Step 4: If unresolved: register with the National Consumer Helpline  [todo]
**What to do:** Register your grievance (NyayaSetu draft C) by calling 1915, on WhatsApp 8800001915, through the NCH or UMANG app, or online.
**Why it matters:** NCH is the Department of Consumer Affairs' pre-litigation grievance channel. Grievances against its partner (convergence) companies are sent to them for resolution, and the portal says it may take up to 30 days to reach a conclusion. If your grievance is not resolved, you can still approach the Consumer Commission. NyayaSetu found no rule in the sources it checked that makes NCH compulsory before going to a Commission.
**Official website:** consumerhelpline.gov.in — https://consumerhelpline.gov.in/
**Evidence to keep:** NCH docket / grievance number, Company's reply via NCH

## Step 5: If still unresolved: file before the appropriate Consumer Commission on e-Jagriti  [todo]
**What to do:** Use NyayaSetu draft D to prepare, then file online on e-Jagriti, where you can upload documents, pay any fee and track the case. You can file yourself; an advocate or a recognised consumer association can also help.
**Why it matters:** The Commission can order a refund, replacement, compensation and costs if it finds in your favour. Complaints must normally be filed within 2 years of the cause of action; no fee applies for value up to ₹5 lakh (check the portal).
**Official website:** e-jagriti.gov.in — https://e-jagriti.gov.in/
**Evidence to keep:** All documents above, indexed as annexures, Copies of your complaints and their replies
